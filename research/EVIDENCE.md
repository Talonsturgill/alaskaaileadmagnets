# The Evidence Base

Compiled 2026-07-21 from fetched pages. Two halves. First, what the credible
research says about who actually wins with AI in business, the honest stat spine
the series teaches from. Second, what the platform data says about how to ship
it on LinkedIn. Every number carries its source. Skepticism labels are part of
the record, quote them with the numbers.

## Part 1, who wins with AI and why

### MIT, The GenAI Divide, State of AI in Business 2025 (Project NANDA, July 2025)

The headline. 95 percent of enterprise GenAI pilots deliver no measurable P&L
impact despite 30 to 40 billion dollars invested. Only about 5 percent of
custom tools reach production.
- https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf
- https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/
- https://www.aigl.blog/state-of-ai-in-business-2025/

What the winning 5 percent do differently.
- Buy, do not build. Purchased or partnered tools reach deployment about 67
  percent of the time. Internal builds succeed about a third as often.
- Back office beats front office. Over half of budgets go to sales and
  marketing, the biggest ROI shows up in back office automation.
- Line managers own adoption, not a central AI lab.
- They cross the learning gap. Winning tools embed in one high value workflow,
  customize deeply, and retain feedback and context. 66 percent of executives
  want systems that learn from feedback, 63 percent demand context retention.
  Generic chatbots drive high adoption but low transformation.

Caveat, always flag when citing. Small sample (52 structured interviews, 153
surveyed leaders, about 300 public deployments), not peer reviewed, and "no P&L
impact within six months" is a strict bar. Widely cited, methodologically
contested. House rule from the sibling repos applies, cite the failure stats
together (MIT, RAND, Gartner, McKinsey), never alone.

### BCG, the 10-20-70 principle and the value gap

- 10-20-70. Only 10 percent of AI effort is algorithms, 20 percent is tech and
  data, 70 percent is people, process, and workflow change.
  https://www.bcg.com/capabilities/artificial-intelligence
  https://www.forbes.com/sites/joemckendrick/2026/01/26/why-ais-10-20-70-principle-should-matter-to-ceos-and-everyone-else/
- Where's the Value in AI (Oct 2024, 1,000 executives, 59 countries). 74 percent
  of companies struggle to achieve and scale AI value. Only 26 percent get
  beyond proof of concept. AI leaders show 1.5x revenue growth and 1.6x
  shareholder returns over three years.
  https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value
- From Potential to Profit (AI Radar, Jan 2025). Leaders concentrate on about
  3.5 use cases versus 6.1 for laggards, put 80 plus percent of AI investment
  into reshaping core functions, and expect 2.1x the ROI. Only 29 percent of
  companies have trained even a quarter of their workforce.
  https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/closing-the-ai-impact-gap

### McKinsey, The State of AI (Nov 5, 2025)

88 percent of organizations use AI in at least one function, up from 78. Only
39 percent attribute any EBIT impact to it, and most of those report under 5
percent of EBIT. High performers (about 6 percent of respondents) are about 3x
more likely to have fundamentally redesigned workflows, 3x more likely to have
visible senior leader ownership, and at least 3x more likely to be scaling
agents.
- https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai
- https://www.cxtoday.com/ai-automation-in-cx/mckinseys-state-of-ai-the-scaling-gap-is-now-cxs-problem/

### Small and mid-market adoption

- US Chamber of Commerce and Teneo, Empowering Small Business (Aug 2025, 3,870
  businesses under 250 employees). 58 percent use generative AI, up from 40 in
  2024 and 23 in 2023, the fastest tech uptake the Chamber has tracked since
  social media. Tech leads at 77 percent, construction (47) and manufacturing
  (46) trail. 82 percent of AI-using small businesses grew headcount in the
  past year.
  https://www.uschamber.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/u-s-chambers-latest-empowering-small-business-report-shows-majority-of-businesses-in-all-50-states-are-embracing-ai
- Thin, cite cautiously. A Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses figure (76
  percent use AI, only 14 percent have it embedded in core operations, 2026)
  circulates via aggregators, primary page not verified.
  https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-guide-2026

### The distilled success factors (the series' recurring spine)

1. Narrow high frequency workflows beat broad platforms (MIT, BCG 3.5 use
   cases).
2. Back office first, that is where measured ROI lives (MIT).
3. Buy and customize beats build from scratch (MIT, about 67 vs about 33
   percent).
4. Workflow redesign is the strongest EBIT correlate (McKinsey 3x).
5. 70 percent of the work is people and process, not the model (BCG).
6. Tools that learn, feedback loops and context retention, win (MIT).
7. Line manager ownership beats a central lab (MIT, McKinsey).
8. Training and data readiness are the bottleneck (BCG 29 percent).

The gap the series bridges, in one line. Adoption is broad and shallow,
most businesses use AI, almost none have wired it into operations, and the
winners are ordinary companies that redesigned one workflow at a time.

## Part 2, LinkedIn mechanics 2025 to 2026

### Format performance

- Company pages (Socialinsider, Mar 2026, 1.3M posts). Native documents lead at
  7.00 percent engagement (up 14 percent YoY), multi image 6.45, video 6.00,
  single image 5.30, text 4.50, link posts last at 3.25.
  https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin
- Personal profiles (AuthoredUp, 3M plus posts, updated Jun 2026). Documents
  1.39x median reach and 1.30x engagement, images 1.20x and 1.33x, text 1.07x
  reach, video 0.86x reach. Polls are a reach trap, 1.78x reach with 0.37x
  engagement.
  https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin
- Buffer (Mar 2026). LinkedIn carousels earn the top median engagement, 196
  percent more than video and 585 percent more than text. Engagement definition
  unstated, use the ranking, not the absolute number.
  https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/
- Metricool (2026 study, 670K plus posts). Page impressions down 10 percent
  YoY, likes down 13, comments down 17, clicks up 5. Carousels out-earn images
  on impressions, 1,234 vs 953 per post.
  https://metricool.com/press-release-linkedin-study-2026/
- Overall organic decline (van der Blom Algorithm InSights 2025, 1.8M posts,
  self published). Views down about 50 percent, engagement down about 25,
  follower growth down about 59. Industry standard, not peer reviewed.
  https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/linkedin/linkedin-algorithm-2025/
  https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/

### Video, genuinely contested

LinkedIn said (Dec 2024) video watch time rose 36 percent YoY and short form
creation grew at 2x other formats. Socialinsider measured video views down 36
percent YoY on pages, AuthoredUp found median video reach down 36 percent on
profiles. Both can be true, supply doubled, per post organic payoff fell. Short
form norm is 30 to 90 seconds.
- https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-shares-video-creation-tips-based-on-platform-trends/821050/
- https://thelonerecruiter.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Mastering-the-LinkedIn-Algorithm-in-202526-.pdf

### Carousel craft (matches the sibling repo's CAROUSEL_CRAFT)

7 to 12 slides, roughly 25 to 50 words per slide (van der Blom, secondhand).
Upload as PDF.
- https://blog.brandghost.ai/posts/how-many-slides-linkedin-carousel/
- https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-carousels/

### Profile vs page, cadence, the golden hour

- Personal beats page, especially for text (0.88x profile vs 0.42x page
  multiplier, documents strong on both at about 1.40x). Strategy implication,
  run the series on the founder's profile, the page is the archive.
  https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm
- Cadence. 3 to 5 posts per week, spaced 24 hours plus, never multiple posts
  per day on one surface. First 60 minutes of engagement determines
  distribution, content now stays in feeds for days.

### Lead generation without cheese

- Links in the post body suppress initial reach. Put links in the first comment
  or run link free posts with a profile level CTA.
- Comment gated resources fit the algorithm mechanically, but the effectiveness
  numbers come from vendors selling DM automation, thin and conflicted, and the
  format increasingly reads as engagement bait, which the platform says it is
  de-prioritizing. For an anti-hype brand, sparing use or none.
  https://connectsafely.ai/articles/linkedin-lead-magnet-guide-2026
- The durable funnel is the profile funnel. Consistent narrow expertise,
  optimized headline and featured section, brand first sequencing (a 2.3x
  claim circulates for brand before conversion, loosely sourced, directional
  only).

### Where evidence is thin

Absolute engagement rates are incomparable across studies (different
denominators). All lead gen tactic numbers are vendor sourced. Van der Blom's
multipliers are pre 2026 and self published. Nothing here is LinkedIn official
except the video growth claims.

## What this means for the series (the synthesis in six lines)

1. Daily carousel (PDF document post) is the right core format, top organic
   format on both pages and profiles, and the house already owns a world class
   carousel engine.
2. Run it on Talon's personal profile, not the Alaska.Ai page, personal reach
   multipliers are about 2x page, and the page already carries the daily news
   deck, so the two dailies never collide on one surface.
3. Video is the phase two flagship, not the daily core, contested organic
   payoff, 10x the production weight, best saved for the strongest stories at
   a weekly cadence.
4. No comment gating gimmicks. First comment carries sources and any link. The
   profile and a standing free offer do the converting.
5. The educational spine writes itself from Part 1, the series teaches the
   documented behaviors of the winning 5 percent, one real company at a time.
6. Post at a consistent time, protect the first hour, end on a real question.
