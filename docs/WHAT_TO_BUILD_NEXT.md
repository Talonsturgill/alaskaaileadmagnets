# What To Build Next, the path to leader of AI in Alaska

A first-principles strategy memo, produced 2026-07-22 by a multi-agent room
(three research briefs on the AI frontier, the Alaska economy, and
productization go-to-market, then five strategist rooms and two independent
red-teams reasoning against a shared digest). This is a decision record, not
a plan of record. It sits beside BRAINSTORM.md as company-level thinking.

House voice applies, no em or en dashes anywhere.

## 1. The question, reframed

The ask was "what do we build next to be the leader of AI in Alaska." The
first move is to reject the premise hidden in "build." Alaska AI today is two
things fused, a media brand (the daily authority voice) and an outbound
agency (the Field Study machine). Both are demand generation. Neither is a
moat. And the person running it is one operator whose only scalable workforce
is autonomous Claude Code routines, which are extraordinary at producing
content and drafts and cannot deliver enterprise builds, hold certifications,
sit in procurement meetings, or answer a 2am support call.

So "leader of AI in Alaska" in the next twelve months cannot mean owning
infrastructure the whole ecosystem routes through. For a solo operator it
means being the single most trusted, most cited, most unavoidable honest name
attached to AI work in the state, with a handful of real delivered
references. You own a reputation and a wedge, then you earn the routing layer
later, on demand, never by betting the farm on it now.

## 2. The one first principle

Foundation models are commoditizing. Inference cost fell roughly 1000x since
2022 and the model gaps are closing, so value has moved up the stack to
workflow, data, trust, and governance. A one-person Anchorage shop will never
out-model the frontier labs and must never try. What the frontier labs will
NOT do is the entire opening, they will not serve a niche vertical with a
small market, build compliance infrastructure for one regulated industry,
integrate into local legacy workflows, or give long-term high-touch support.

The uncopyable moats for an Alaska firm are three, proprietary local
KNOWLEDGE fused to delivery, local TRUST plus data SOVEREIGNTY plus
institutional relationships, and on-the-ground presence for physically hard
Alaska problems. And the sharpest asset is already built and currently wasted.

THE GOLDEN THREAD. The honesty engine, claim-ids and every fact tracing to a
fetched page, is structurally identical to Abridge's "Linked Evidence," the
auditability that let a 5.3 billion dollar vertical-AI company beat incumbents
who could not simply add AI. Alaska AI runs that machine today, editorially
only. The whole strategy is to productize auditability into DELIVERY. An AI
that shows its receipts is the one thing a Lower-48 cloud cannot give a
sovereignty-bound, compliance-bound, or health buyer. Point the honesty
machine at delivery and it stops being a brand value and becomes the moat.

## 3. The binding constraint

It is not leads. Four routines flood the top of the funnel at near-zero cost.
The constraint is Talon's attention and delivery capacity. Demand generation
is effectively unbounded, delivery is one human's calendar, realistically
three to six real production engagements a year, and a single deep build can
eat six months. Every new build competes for that one attention pool and
therefore raises the marginal cost of authority, which is currently near
zero. That near-zero is the golden goose. Protect it above everything.

The corollary that disciplines the whole roadmap, more qualified leads
against a fixed delivery ceiling can be NEGATIVE value. So the scarce resource
to optimize is revenue and moat per unit of Talon's attention, not reach.

## 4. What the whole room agreed on (the discipline)

1. Never compete on model quality.
2. Protect the content plus Field Study machine. No new build is worth
   starving it. Every move must be near-zero attention or self-funding.
3. The honesty engine is the Abridge moat. Productize it into delivery.
4. The binding constraint is attention and delivery, not leads.
5. The Docket graduates to an annual "State of AI in Alaska" report, an
   authored publication, NOT a live public queryable graph (clone-able off
   public data, a staleness liability that would invert the honesty brand,
   no regional-tracker-to-revenue precedent, and Alaskan Intelligence LLC
   already stakes the civic-data claim).
6. Model-transfer pricing, "you own the model," is the structure that lets a
   solo operator deliver and leave instead of deliver and support.
7. Keep CUI, on-prem, and certification out of every v1. Start with the
   non-regulated front mile every time.
8. Graduate the capital-heavy bets only on demand pull, never thesis push.

## 6. The roadmap

### Horizon 1, now to about six months. Monetize and compound the machine you
have. No farm bets.

MOVE 0, PROTECT. Keep the content and Field Study routines running untouched.
Non-negotiable. Every move below is near-zero attention or self-funding.

MOVE 1, THE KEYSTONE, the paid rung. Package the free Field Study into a PAID,
fixed-scope, deliver-in-days diagnostic or readiness product, roughly 2k to
15k dollars for the diagnostic, or a 15k to 40k dollar paid pilot with the
model transferred. This is packaging, not new software, the Field Study
machine already renders the artifact at near-zero cost. It gives the funnel
the paid, deliverable next step it structurally lacks, and it monetizes proven
authority before any capital is committed. Model-transfer pricing means
deliver and leave. Do this first because it relieves the actual binding
constraint and needs nothing built.

MOVE 2, the Bottleneck Scanner as the cheap front door. Ship the thinnest
version, a URL box that writes one row to Supabase and triggers a stripped
leadflow-style routine that fetches the business's public footprint, maps it
onto the existing Anchorage bottleneck map and the feasibility ladder, and
renders ONE honest laddered map of where AI would and would not help, as a
commit-pinned page, then a consented opt-in that seeds a human-gated Field
Study. It renders the "do you even need AI here" gate as a public tool a
national competitor cannot fake, and every scan is first-party demand-side
data. Ship it AFTER the paid rung exists so qualified intent converts to money
rather than to an unserved queue. The risk is honesty at scale, one bad public
reading is self-served to the exact operator you wanted, so gate it hard, a
study-critic defaulting to reject, a weekly Talon audit of real scans, ranges
not hero numbers, and a rule that the free map describes bottlenecks and never
promises outcomes.

MOVE 3, capture the exhaust, the private intelligence graph. Add one
write-back step to the routines that already run, landing structured nodes and
edges (company, vendor, deployment, lease, bottleneck, price, outcome,
decision, each with a claim-id and a fetched source) into a new Supabase
schema. The Field Study generator then READS the graph, so every study places
the prospect inside the whole Alaska buildout, the vendor landscape,
comparable deployments, and the procurement gate they will hit. This is the
generative moat, source-restricted first-party data tied to a workflow, the
one condition under which data is actually defensible, and it compounds at the
rate the machine already runs for near-zero added effort. It is NOT a public
graph.

MOVE 4, graduate the Docket to an annual "State of AI in Alaska" report. Run
the graph through the existing render machine once a year to author the
report the ecosystem cites. Authored authority at content-machine cost, not a
live service.

CROSS-CUTTING, pick the soil, not the crop yet. Tilt content and free Field
Studies toward the two highest-value lanes, ANC and 8(a) compliance-adjacent
work and independent-healthcare back office, to accumulate pattern knowledge
and relationships. Do not pre-build a delivery engine. Let paid conversions
reveal which lane earns productization.

### Horizon 2, about six to eighteen months. Demand-gated platform bets. Enter
each ONLY on demand pull, never thesis push.

THE PRODUCTIZED DELIVERY ENGINE, in whichever lane the paid conversions
proved. Templatize the C4 skeleton, leadflow's two rooms repointed from
"Field Study for a prospect" to "readiness package for a client," targeting
70 to 80 percent reuse per build. The research favored ANC 8(a) proposal and
compliance, the buyer can fund the productization, CMMC fences out national
public-cloud SaaS, and the buyer set is small enough to own by name.
Independent healthcare is the second lane. Enter ONLY after roughly three to
five same-type paid model-transfer wins with a waitlist, when delivery not
demand is the constraint. This is the trigger for the first hire or
subcontract. Keep auditability as the headline feature, in compliance it is
contract eligibility, not convenience.

THE SOVEREIGNTY STANDARD. Author it CHEAP NOW as a content and authority
asset, a governance framework plus a compliant reference architecture,
published through the machine that exists, staking the claim before an ANC IT
subsidiary or Alaskan Intelligence does. Pursue it INSTITUTIONALLY, co-author
with UAA's RAISE consortium and a governance body, one partner-carried
lighthouse pilot, ONLY with a partner who brings the CMMC certification, the
capital, and the procurement seat (DenaliTek, CIRI's OSC Edge, Koniag). Never
fund the institutional cycle from content revenue. Alaska AI is the architect
and auditor, the partner is the custodian and integrator.

THE CUI AND ON-PREM COMPLIANCE TIER. Only against a named, funded contract,
delivered WITH a certified partner-custodian, never solo. This is where the
"model they own" promise reaches its ceiling.

## 7. The do-not-build-yet list (explicit)

- No public queryable ask-anything intelligence graph. Clone-able off public
  data, staleness liability, no revenue path, competitor already there.
- No CMMC, CUI, or on-prem delivery solo. Uncertified, the cloud routines are
  barred from CUI, and Koniag, DenaliTek, and OSC Edge are already certified
  and staffed in that lane.
- No attempt to BE the sovereignty or governance layer as a primary bet.
  Institutional time, needs organizational durability a solo shop cannot
  attest to.
- No managed retainers on systems Talon must personally support. Each one is a
  permanent lien on the scarce resource.
- No second vertical until the first has about three paying clients.
- No hiring until turned-away qualified demand exceeds the solo ceiling for two
  or more quarters, with reserves to cover a bad hire.
- Never compete on model quality.

## 8. Metrics, winning versus busy

Leading indicators of WINNING. The unprompted "I saw your carousel" or "I got
your Field Study" reference rate exceeding outbound. Paid diagnostics sold per
month and their close rate from the Field Study. Field-Study-to-paid
conversion and a FALLING time-to-yes. Revenue per engagement rising and
support hours per engagement falling toward zero. Reference density, the count
of named honest local wins. Third-party citations of the annual report.

Vanity to distrust. Followers, impressions, routines shipped, scanner
sessions, Docket completeness, raw citation counts unpaired with revenue.

## 9. Graduation triggers (demand pull, never thesis push)

- Productized delivery engine and first hire, after three or more profitable
  same-type model-transfer builds with near-zero support burden and a waitlist.
- Sovereignty or institutional play, only when a named institution puts money
  or a signed pilot on the table AND a partner holds the cert and support
  posture.
- A real Docket or intelligence product, only on demand pull from a paying
  institution, or when the annual report has paying sponsors.
- CMMC and on-prem infrastructure, only against a named ANC contract with a
  real dollar figure, delivered with an existing certified partner.

## 10. The one-line answer

Do not build a new machine yet. Add the paid rung the machine is missing,
point the honesty engine at delivery, capture the exhaust into a private moat,
publish the report the state will cite, and let the world ask for the builds.
Spend capital on delivery scale only after demand pulls harder than one person
can serve. That is the only version of "leader of AI in Alaska" a solo
operator can actually hold, and it is the one that compounds into the rest.
