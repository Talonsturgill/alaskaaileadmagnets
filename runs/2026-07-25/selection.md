## The thesis

A clinic network published a striking number about its own AI scribe. The
peer reviewed literature on the same capability published a much more modest
one. Both are honestly arrived at, they measure different things, and the
operator buying this in 2026 should plan against the modest one. Teaching a
reader how to read two disagreeing numbers is worth more to them than a
fourth AI success story, and it is the reason to believe the next case file.

This stays a CASE FILE, not a reality check. One company, one bottleneck, one
build, receipts, what it took, the catch, the translation. The independent
evidence lives where it belongs, in the receipts and the catch.

   Verbatim on the page today: "On average, the system generates a complete

2. Independent trade corroboration of the launch exists.

3. The independent peer reviewed counterweight exists and is strong.
   Olson KD, Meeker D, Troup M, Barker TD, Nguyen VH, Manders JB, Stults CD,
   Jones VG, Shah SD, Shah T, Schwamm LH. "Use of Ambient AI Scribes to
   Reduce Administrative Burden and Professional Burnout." JAMA Network Open.
   2025;8(10):e2534976. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.34976.
   Multicenter quality improvement study, 6 academic and community health
   systems, February 1 to October 31 2024, 451 clinicians enrolled, 272
   completed both surveys, 263 analyzed. Burnout fell from 51.9 percent to
   38.8 percent after 30 days, odds ratio 0.26, 95 percent CI 0.13 to 0.54.
   After hours documentation mean difference 0.90 hours.

## Landmine the fact-checker must defuse

A summary circulating about the JAMA study states the scribe "reduced daily
documentation by nearly 7 minutes and afterhours work by 5 minutes". That
framing was NOT read on a fetched primary page during selection, and its
units do not obviously reconcile with the abstract's "0.90 hours" figure.
Treat the 7 minute and 5 minute figures as UNVERIFIED and do not let them
into claims.json unless the JAMA article itself states them with units. If
they cannot be pinned exactly, the deck uses the burnout result and the
after hours result as published and makes no per chart minutes comparison
against Carbon Health at all. The contrast still lands, because the honest
point is that the independent study measured burnout and after hours load
rather than a per chart speedup.
