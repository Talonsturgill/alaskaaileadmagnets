## The best thing wave one found

**Grand Island Express, Inc.** (Grand Island, Nebraska, refrigerated truckload
carrier hauling swinging beef to 38 states, founded 1967). Bought Optimal
Dynamics, fed it live data from their existing McLeod Loadmaster TMS, and it now
recommends which driver takes which load while a dispatcher accepts or
overrides. Partnership January 2024, live April 2024. VP of Operations Deen
Albert on the record: "We were dispatching in a reactionary type of format."

The number that makes this unusual: **50 percent of loads matched the
platform's recommendations in week one, 84 percent from week two onward and
maintained.** `knowledge/kb/DEPLOYMENT_PATTERNS.md` notes that almost nobody
publishes an accept-versus-override rate, and that rate is exactly the number
that teaches Dana what a rung-4 human-in-the-loop system actually feels like to
run.

It is a clean rung 4, not a rung 5. The steps are fixed and human-designed, the
system does not choose its own tools or plan its own sequence, and a dispatcher
approves the expensive action.

**It failed the hard gate on cost and filter 1 was unproven on headcount**, so
it went to a dedicated gap chase rather than into the bank as a case.

## GROUND INTELLIGENCE (the durable output, for the knowledge base)

### The structural finding, stated by three scouts independently

**Every ground has exactly one half of what we need, and it is never the same
half.**

- Trade press and regional journals: the operator is named, the bottleneck is
  named, the number is named, **the price is absent.** Confirmed across seven
  verticals in one run.
- Public record, grants, MEP: **the cost is public and easy, and the AI
  deployment is missing.** Money documents name a company and an amount and
  disclose almost nothing about what was built or whether it reached
  production.
- Vendor libraries: the build is described, and both cost and independent
  corroboration are absent.

The bank's zero-cost problem is therefore structural, not an artifact of lazy
past runs.

### SEO poisoning, and it is new

Generic operator cost phrases no longer work as queries. "what it cost us", "we
pay about", "$X a month", "paid for itself", "we spent about", "invested about"
now return almost pure AI-vendor listicle content. Vendors are optimising for
exactly the operator language `HUNTING_GROUNDS.md` tells us to search. One scout
measured it: roughly 14 of 20 searches returned zero fetchable operator pages.
Two other scouts hit the same wall independently across seven more attempts.

**The word "AI" poisons a cost query outright.** Search the bottleneck and the
dollar figure with no AI word, then find the AI inside the article.

**What replaced it:** company-name-first beats disclosure-phrase-first. Find the
deployment by sector plus named role, then chase the company by name across
multiple publications.

### Domain constraint is the single highest-yield technique

Every usable lead across all four wave-one scouts came from a domain-constrained
search. Unconstrained searches produced nothing usable in any ground.

- MEP and extension AI explainer blogs (IMEC, Catalyst Connection, WMEP, MAGNET,

  and an ERP upgrade. Zero AI.

  to an entity Dana cannot be is still a kill.

### The agent-washing observation, worth putting in the curriculum

Of everything read across four scouts, the only deployments carrying "agentic"
in the headline were the least specific, naming zero companies, zero numbers and
zero costs. **The deployments with real measured numbers called themselves
nothing at all.** That inverse relationship between the agentic label and the
presence of a measured number is itself a finding Dana would recognise, and it
is a better teaching device than any vendor comparison table.

### The tense check, now a standing intake rule

Wave one killed at least three otherwise-good stories because every number was a
projection stated ahead of launch. Packer Fastener's 75 percent of quotes
automated and 40 hours a month were expectations. CJB's batch-sheet AI was
"expects to convert". Extension-program success stories in particular get
written when the engagement ends, which is usually before the AI reaches
production. **Read the verbs. "Expects to", "will", "anticipated" and
"projected" are kills, not numbers.**
