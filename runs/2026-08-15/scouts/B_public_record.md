## The ground's central finding (goes to the knowledge base)

This ground has the OPPOSITE problem from every other ground. Elsewhere the
story is good and the cost is missing. **Here the cost is public and easy, and
the AI deployment is missing.** Money documents name a company and an amount and
disclose almost nothing about what was built or whether it reached production.

1. State Industry 4.0 and Manufacturing 4.0 grants fund overwhelmingly CAPITAL
   EQUIPMENT. One 500,000 dollar Iowa award fetched in detail was robotic weld
   cells, a plasma cutter, a CNC brake press and an ERP upgrade. Zero AI.
   "Industry 4.0" in state grant language means metal-cutting machines with
   network ports.
2. MEP impact reporting is aggregate by construction. Rhode Island's 2025 report
   gives 403 jobs and 2.55 million dollars across 169 companies and names none.
3. MEP AI content is educational, not case-based. IMEC, Catalyst Connection,
   WMEP, MAGNET, The Center all publish explainers with borrowed McKinsey and
   Deloitte percentages and no named client. The NRECA AI advisory PDF, fetched
   in full, had no deployment, no vendor, no cost, no quote.
4. Where a named AI story exists here, the AI is in FUTURE TENSE. Twice.
   Extension success stories get written when the engagement ends, which is
   usually before the AI reaches production.
5. Municipal and port board packets are a trap regardless of yield. A perfect
   cost disclosure attached to an entity Dana cannot be is still a kill.

## What to try next time on this ground

- **A.** Mine the recipient PDFs directly with pypdf rather than WebFetch. The
  Maryland Manufacturing 4.0 recipient list (43 named companies with project
  descriptions) and the Iowa IEDA awards PDF are both unmined. Screen the
  project descriptions for "software", "vision", "machine learning", NOT for
  "AI", then take survivors to the trade press for the operator story.
- **B.** Search extension centers for the FOLLOW-UP story, company name plus a
  later year. Production numbers land 12 to 24 months after the case study.
- **C.** Electric cooperatives are the best untested sub-ground and map hard onto
  Anchorage. Individual co-op board packets and statewide association magazines,
  never NRECA centrally.
- **D.** Stop searching "AI" on extension sites. Search the bottleneck plus the
  center name.

## Query craft that FAILED (record it so nobody repeats it)

- Procurement language ("not to exceed", "contract award", "staff report") plus
  AI returns municipal agenda items, disqualified on reader fit before reading.
- "final report" plus "total project cost" plus AI returns grant landing pages
  and AI pricing content marketing.
- Operator disclosure phrases ("we spent about", "cost us about", "invested
  about") returned ZERO real operator quotes across three attempts here, because
  grant and extension documents are written in institutional voice. That
  disclosure language belongs to the conference-talk and podcast grounds.
