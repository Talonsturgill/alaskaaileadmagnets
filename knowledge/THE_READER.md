# THE READER

**This is the most important file in the repository. Every agent reads it
first. Every artifact is judged against it. If an agent is ever unsure what to
do, the answer is whatever serves the person described below.**

Nothing here is about us. It is about one specific person, and our only job is
to be useful to them.

---

## Who they are

Dana runs a 240 person company in Anchorage.

Pick whichever version you like, because they are all the same person. A
mechanical contractor with 180 field techs and a dispatch board. A seafood
processor that triples headcount in July. A regional freight outfit with 90
trucks and three yards. An ANC subsidiary bidding federal work. A 40 provider
clinic group. A civil engineering firm with a permitting backlog. A tour
operator whose whole year happens between May and September.

Dana is not a technologist. Dana is the owner, the CEO, or the person the CEO
turns to when something has to actually get done. They have a P&L, a payroll
that clears every two weeks, and a phone that rings when a customer is angry.

**What Dana knows about AI:** they have used ChatGPT. Somebody on their team
uses it for emails. They have sat through at least one vendor demo with a big
number on a slide. They have read that AI is going to change everything and
also that most AI projects fail. Both of those statements are useless to them.

**What Dana does not know, and cannot easily find out:**
- What an actual working deployment looks like at their size
- What it costs, really, including the parts nobody quotes
- Whether "agent" means anything or is just a word vendors use now
- Which of their own problems is even the right one to point this at
- Whether anyone even remotely like them has pulled this off

**The feeling we are addressing:** Dana is not skeptical. Dana is
*overwhelmed*. There is a difference and it changes everything about how we
write. A skeptic needs proof. An overwhelmed person needs a **door** — one
specific, small, real thing that someone their size actually did, told
concretely enough that they can picture themselves doing it.

Dana does not need to be convinced that AI matters. Dana needs to be shown
where to put their hands.

---

## The one reaction we are engineering

Every single post exists to produce this sentence in Dana's head:

> **"Huh. I could do that."**

Not "that's interesting." Not "good to know." Not "these people seem smart."

**"I could do that."**

That sentence has four ingredients and a post fails if it is missing any one
of them:

1. **A company Dana recognizes.** Same size, same ordinariness, same kind of
   unglamorous problem. If the company is a tech firm, a Fortune 500, or a
   startup with venture money, Dana files it under "not me" and stops reading.
2. **A problem Dana feels in their body.** Not "inefficiency." The estimator
   who takes three days to turn a bid around. The 400 invoices a month that
   get keyed by hand. The night shift supervisor who writes the same report
   every morning at 5am.
3. **A move Dana can picture making.** Bought, configured, pointed at one
   thing. If the answer is "they built it in house with a data science team,"
   Dana cannot copy it and the post is entertainment.
4. **A number Dana can carry into a meeting.** Something they could repeat to
   their CFO on Thursday.

## THE SIX QUESTIONS

**Every agent, at every step, applies this gate to whatever it just produced.**
This is not a checklist for the end. It is the lens you look through while you
work. A scout applies it to a candidate. A copywriter applies it to a line. A
pixel critic applies it to a slide.

Read your own output and answer honestly:

1. **Does Dana see themselves in this company?**
   Size, industry texture, ordinariness. Would Dana say "that's basically us"?

2. **Does the problem sound like Dana's Tuesday?**
   Is it named in operator language, with the friction described the way
   somebody who lives it would describe it?

3. **Can Dana picture actually doing this?**
   Is the move small, specific, and available to a 240 person company with no
   AI staff? Do we say what it cost and how long it took?

4. **Is there a number that makes Dana sit up?**
   One figure, concrete, that survives being repeated out loud.

5. **Does Dana learn something about AI they did not know?**
   Especially about agentic systems. Does the fog get thinner? Can they now
   tell one kind of AI from another?

6. **Would Dana send this to their ops lead?**
   The honest test of whether it was worth their attention.

**If any answer is no, the work is not finished. Fix it or kill it.**

## What this rules out, permanently

These are not style preferences. They are the failure modes that made previous
runs miss.

**A post about evidence quality instead of a use case.** We had a deck arguing
that a per visit number cannot be compared to a per week number. It was
rigorous and it was correct and Dana learned nothing they could act on. Our
sourcing discipline is *plumbing*. It makes the claim trustworthy. It is never
the subject.

**A company Dana cannot be.** A venture backed clinic chain that built its own
AI inside an electronic health record it owns is not a peer. It is a different
species. The size band is 50 to 1,000 employees and the company must be
recognizably ordinary.

**A win Dana cannot copy.** If the company hired ML engineers, the story is
closed to Dana. We want bought, configured, wired up, pointed at one job.

**A case with no cost and no timeline.** This is the hardest new rule and the
most important. If we cannot tell Dana roughly what it cost and roughly how
long it took, **the case does not qualify.** Not "we ship it without that
slide." It does not qualify. That information is the difference between a
story and a plan.

**Hedging that eats the story.** Honesty is absolute and non negotiable, and
it lives *inside the sentence*, not in its own slide. "Per the company's own
numbers, quote turnaround went from three days to four hours" is honest,
labeled, and exciting all at once. Four slides of methodology is not honesty,
it is fear.

**Fear as a motivator.** We never sell with "you are falling behind." Dana has
been shouted at by enough marketing. We show a door and let them walk through.

## The agentic mandate

Dana keeps hearing the word "agent" and has no idea what it means. Everyone is
using it and nobody is defining it. This is the single largest fog bank between
Dana and useful action, and clearing it is the most valuable thing we do.

**So the series is not just "AI use cases." It is a curriculum in what these
systems actually are, taught one real deployment at a time.**

Every case file names where the build sits on the feasibility ladder (see
`knowledge/AGENTIC_LITERACY.md`) in plain words, so that over months of
reading, Dana develops something no vendor will give them: **the ability to
tell a chatbot from a workflow from a genuine agent, and to know which one
their problem needs.**

Bias the hunt toward the frontier. Multi step agentic workflows, systems that
use tools, systems that plan their own steps, human in the loop checkpoints.
Not because it is fashionable, but because it is where Dana's confusion is
thickest and where the honest teaching is worth the most.

A post that leaves Dana able to say "oh, THAT is what an agent is" has done
something no competitor is doing for them.

---

## Voice, in one paragraph

Talk to Dana the way a good operator talks to another operator over coffee.
Short sentences. Specific nouns. The number said plainly with its source in
the same breath. No hype, no hedging, no throat clearing, no consultant fog.
Respect Dana's intelligence and their calendar. Assume they are smart, busy,
and have been marketed at for three years by people who did not do the work.

We are not selling. We are handing over something we found.

---

## The standard

This series is not content. It is a **research product**.

Our advantage is that we can send researchers into the world and come back
with the specific, small, real wins that are actually happening at Dana's
scale, which nobody is aggregating and which Dana has no way to find alone.
Then we translate one honestly into something Dana can use.

Being a sniper is the job. One excellent, specific, verified, exciting case
beats ten competent surveys. Every time.
