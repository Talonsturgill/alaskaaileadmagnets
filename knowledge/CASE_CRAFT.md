# CASE CRAFT

The story grammar. Read `knowledge/THE_READER.md` and
`knowledge/AGENTIC_LITERACY.md` first, in that order. This file owns how a
verified win becomes a post that makes Dana say **"I could do that."**

`CAROUSEL_CRAFT.md` owns platform physics. `DESIGN_DOCTRINE.md` owns the visual
standard. `SLIDE_DOSSIER_SPEC.md` owns the planning format.
`TECHNIQUE_LIBRARY.md` owns the art.

## THE QUALIFYING FILTER

A case ships only if **all seven** hold. This runs before anything else. It is
cheaper to kill a case at intake than to discover on slide six that it was
never Dana's story.

1. **50 to 1,000 employees, and not a technology company.** A contractor, a
   processor, a distributor, a clinic group, a manufacturer, a hauler, a firm.
   Dana must be able to say "that's basically us."
2. **ONE named bottleneck, in operator language.** "Estimators took three days
   to turn a bid" qualifies. "Operational inefficiency" does not.
3. **They bought or configured it. They did not build it from scratch.** If the
   answer required hiring ML engineers, Dana cannot copy it. Buying, wiring up,
   and pointing something at one job is the shape we want.
4. **A measurable before and after, with a real number.** From X to Y. Not
   "improved efficiency," not "unlocked value."
5. **Cost class AND timeline are known.** Roughly what it cost, roughly how
   long from decision to working. **This is a hard gate.** If we cannot tell
   Dana what it took, the case does not qualify, no matter how good the story
   is. This is the rule that most changes what we ship.
6. **A named human said it on the record.** An owner, an ops lead, a plant
   manager. A quote with a name on it.
7. **2024 or later**, unless the win is structurally durable and we say why.
   Tooling economics moved too fast for older numbers to guide a decision now.

**Preference order when several cases qualify:** the more agentic build wins
(it teaches more of the ladder), then the more ordinary company, then the
better documented cost and timeline, then the sector that maps hardest onto
Anchorage.

**Geography:** the lower 48 is the default hunting ground. Alaska is too thin a
market to be a filter and hunting only here starves the bank. Alaska is where
the story *lands*, not where it must be found. An Alaska case is a bonus, never
a requirement.

## THE GRAMMAR — 8 slides

Eight is the default. Seven to ten is legal when the dossier defends it. Every
slide leans forward. There is no slide whose job is to defend us.

**1. COVER — the hook**
The number and the ordinary company, in twelve words or fewer. Huge type.
Dana should be able to tell, at thumb size, that this is about a company like
theirs and that something concrete happened.
*Test:* would Dana stop scrolling and would they know why?

**2. THE COMPANY — identification**
Who, where, how many people, how old, what they actually do. Deliberately
unglamorous. Family owned, 1974, 210 employees, four yards.
*This slide's only job is to make Dana think "that's basically us."* It comes
before any technology word. If Dana does not identify here, nothing downstream
lands.

**3. THE BOTTLENECK — what it was costing**
The specific pocket of pain, in operator terms, with its cost named. Hours,
dollars, missed jobs, turnover, delayed cash. Quote the operator when we have
a fetched quote.
*Test:* does this sound like Dana's Tuesday?

**4. THE BUILD — what they actually did**
The mechanism, in plain talk, concrete enough to picture. What they bought,
what it plugged into, what it does step by step, where the human sits.
**Name the ladder rung here** in plain words, per `AGENTIC_LITERACY.md`. Name
vendors — we are not afraid of naming tools we do not sell.
*Test:* can Dana picture the thing running in their building?

**5. THE RECEIPTS — the numbers**
The keepable slide. Before and after, tabular, every figure carrying its
source in the same breath. This is the slide Dana screenshots and brings to
their CFO.
*Test:* could Dana repeat one of these out loud on Thursday?

**6. WHAT IT TOOK — the plan**
**Required. Never cut. This is the slide that separates us from everyone
else.** What it cost as a range, how long from decision to working, who owned
it internally, what had to be true first (data, systems, permissions), and
what the ninety day ramp looked like.
*Test:* does Dana now know what they would be signing up for?

**7. YOUR VERSION — the Anchorage translation**
Which Anchorage segments have this exact bottleneck, what the local version of
this build looks like, roughly what it would cost here, and **where it does not
apply.** Segments, never a named local company as a target.
*Test:* does Dana see their own operation in this?

**8. CLOSE — one ask**
The configured offer line from `config/brand.yaml`, exactly once, exactly as
written. The mark, the site, sources in comments. Educational, zero pressure.

**The three that can never be cut:** THE RECEIPTS, WHAT IT TOOK, and YOUR
VERSION. Those three are the series. A deck missing any one of them is not a
case file.

## THE EXCITEMENT STANDARD

Honesty is the floor. Excitement is the target. A post can be perfectly
truthful and completely inert, and an inert post has failed.

**Where excitement legitimately comes from:**
- **Specificity.** "Four hours instead of three days" beats "significantly
  faster." Real nouns beat categories.
- **Recognition.** The pleasure of seeing your own problem described precisely
  by someone who clearly understands it.
- **Scale match.** "A 210 person company did this" is thrilling in a way that
  "a global enterprise did this" never is.
- **Smallness of the move.** The best reaction is "wait, that's *all* they
  did?" The gap between how small the intervention was and how big the result
  was is the entire engine.
- **A door.** Dana finishing the deck knowing the first thing they would do
  Monday.

**Where excitement must never come from:** inflating a number, dropping a
label, hiding a limit, implying a result we did not verify, or fear.

**The tension, resolved:** when the honest version is less exciting, we ship
the honest version *and go find a better case*. We never inflate. But we also
never accept that a case is boring simply because it is honest. If it reads
inert, the case was weak or the telling was lazy. Both are fixable, and neither
is fixed by hedging harder.

## HONESTY, and exactly where it lives

Absolute, and invisible. It rides inside the sentence.

- Vendor number carries its label in the same breath. *"Per the company's own
  case study, quote turnaround went from three days to four hours."* One
  sentence, honest and exciting at once.
- Never invent anything. Every fact traces to a page fetched this run.
- Freed hours are capacity until a decision turns them into cash. Say which.
- Every case names one honest limit, on the YOUR VERSION slide, framed as
  useful scoping rather than as a disclaimer.

**What honesty is NOT allowed to become:** its own slide, its own section, the
subject of the post, or a reason the deck has no cost figure. If our sourcing
discipline is visible to Dana as a *topic*, we have failed. It should be felt
only as trust.

## CAPTION CRAFT

The caption teases and the deck delivers. Keep one load bearing number for the
deck.

- **Hook patterns that work:** the ordinary company with a number ("A 210
  person mechanical contractor cut bid turnaround from three days to four
  hours."), the smallness reversal ("They pointed it at one job. That was the
  whole project."), the recognition open ("Their estimators were the
  bottleneck. Everyone's estimators are the bottleneck.").
- **Identification before information.** Company shape before technology name.
- The offer line appears once, near the close, exactly as configured.
- Sources and URLs live ONLY in the first comment. Never in the post body.
- End with a real question an operator could answer from their own experience.
  War story invitations out-engage generic questions.

## THE KILL LIST

Any one of these means the work is not done.

- **A post that teaches evidence handling instead of showing a win.** Our
  methodology is plumbing. If it becomes the subject, we have written for
  ourselves instead of for Dana.
- **A company Dana cannot be.** Tech firms, Fortune 500s, venture backed
  darlings, research labs. Legal only as a labeled mechanism case, and even
  then only when nothing at Dana's scale exists.
- **A win Dana cannot copy.** Built in house with a data team. Closed door.
- **A case with no cost and no timeline.** Does not qualify. Not a slide we
  skip, a case we do not take.
- **Unlabeled vendor numbers.** The label rides with the number, every time.
- **A sweeping transformation arc.** "They transformed the business" is banned
  energy. "They fixed the bid queue" is the voice.
- **Agent washing, uncorrected.** If a vendor calls a rung 4 workflow an agent
  and we repeat it, we have made Dana's fog thicker instead of thinner.
- **A missing limit.** If we cannot find where it stops working, we have not
  researched enough to ship.
- **Fear.** Never "you are falling behind."
- Hype verbs, dashes, colons, emojis, and the banned lists in `brand.yaml`.

**And the final gate, which sits above all of the above:** read the finished
deck as Dana. If the honest reaction is "that was well made" rather than
**"I could do that,"** it does not ship, no matter how clean it is.
