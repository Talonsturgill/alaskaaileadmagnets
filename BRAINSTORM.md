# The Lead Magnet Series, Brainstorm and Recommendation

Written 2026-07-21 after a full sweep of the three sibling repos, the live site,
and fresh web research (see research/). This is the thinking. The blueprint in
docs/ is the proposed machine. The decision list at the bottom is what the
maintainer picks before anything gets built.

## 1. The goal, said sharp

A daily LinkedIn post that makes an Anchorage mid-market operator stop and
think, that company looks like mine, and they actually did it. Not enterprise
theater. Not hype. Everyday companies winning with TARGETED builds, applied AI
in specific pockets and bottlenecks, with receipts. The post gives the whole
lesson away. The reader who wants their own version knows exactly who to ask.
Showing instead of asking is the entire sales strategy.

## 2. The editorial thesis (and the research agrees)

Ordinary companies. One bottleneck. A targeted build. Real numbers.

This is not a content angle, it is what the credible research actually found
(research/EVIDENCE.md). MIT's GenAI Divide, the 5 percent who get P&L impact
embed in ONE high value workflow and go deep. BCG, leaders concentrate on about
3.5 use cases while laggards scatter across 6 plus. McKinsey, workflow redesign
is the strongest EBIT correlate. US Chamber, 58 percent of small businesses now
use AI, almost none have it wired into operations. Adoption is broad and
shallow. The gap between "we use ChatGPT" and "we rebuilt the intake workflow
and it pays" is exactly the gap this series bridges, one company at a time.

The differentiation no generic AI influencer can copy is the second half of
every post, the Alaska translation. A fish counting model, a plow route
optimizer, a clinic intake engine is interesting. "Here is what the equivalent
looks like for a Kenai charter operator, honestly, including whether you even
need AI for it" is ours alone.

## 3. What we already own (the unfair advantages)

- A proven daily studio architecture. The carousel repo runs a 14 phase
  showrunner with bounded specialist agents, ledger enforced variety, verified
  claims files, pixel critics, honest scoring, and a paste ready Gmail draft.
  The whole pattern is topic agnostic and reusable.
- A world class rendering engine. carousel-engine turns bespoke coded slides
  into 1080x1350 PNGs plus a vector text PDF with machine QA. LinkedIn's top
  organic format is the PDF document post. We already ship those daily.
- The honest engineering method. The private leadflow repo runs a real product
  process, the do-you-even-need-AI gate, the feasibility ladder, capacity vs
  cash ROI, ranges never hero numbers. Its public safe frames become the
  series' teaching spine, and no competitor talks like this.
- The publication. alaskaaihq.com already presents as a daily publication and
  an AI studio with the Docket as its public tracker. This series adds a second
  desk to the same newsroom, the Docket covers the AI beat, the new desk
  teaches the AI playbook.
- Video machinery for later. The weekly repo's Dispatch engine (VO, kinetic
  captions, loudness gated mix, 4x5 master) is a phase two flagship option,
  about 10x the weight of a carousel run, so it earns weekly at most.
- The voice. Blunt, specific, receipts first, zero AI tells, zero cheese,
  enforced by lint and kill lists that already exist.

## 4. The format call

Daily core, a carousel shipped as a PDF document post. The platform evidence is
unambiguous (research/EVIDENCE.md part 2), documents are the top organic format
on both pages and profiles across three independent 2026 studies, while video's
per post organic payoff is falling as supply doubles. And our carousel cost per
run is low on proven machinery.

Weekly flagship, phase two, the best case of the week as a 60 second Dispatch
style video. Only after the daily series has legs.

Surface, the founder's personal profile, with the Alaska.Ai page reposting as
the archive. Personal reach multipliers run about double the page's, and the
page already carries the daily news deck, so the two dailies never collide on
one surface. This also fits the offer, people reply to a person, not a page.

## 5. The concept menu

### A. THE CASE FILE (the recommended core)

One real company per post. The story grammar, locked as slide dossier bones,

1. Cover. The hook, a number or a tension, twelve words or fewer. "A 90
   person cannery taught a camera to count fish."
2. The company. Who, where, size. Deliberately ordinary. The reader must think,
   that is basically us.
3. The bottleneck. The specific pocket, in operational terms, the thing that
   ate hours or leaked money.
4. The build. What they actually built or bought, mechanism in plain talk, on
   the feasibility ladder. No vendor worship, no jargon.
5. The receipts. The numbers slide, keepable, sourced. Saves are the metric
   that matters and keepable data slides drive saves at roughly 5x.
6. What it took. Time, data readiness, people, rough cost class. The
   implementation reality every other AI post skips. This slide IS the bridge
   from capability to implementation.
7. The catch. What would break, where the approach stops working, when you do
   not need AI at all. The honesty slide, our signature.
8. The Alaska translation. Which Anchorage industry twin this speaks to and
   what the equivalent build honestly looks like here.
9. Close. One ask, the standing offer line, the site.

### B. CAPABILITY FILE (recurring flavor, roughly weekly)

One capability demystified through the companies using it. Fine tuning, small
models, RAG over your own files, vision counting, forecasting, route
optimization. Structure, what it is in plain talk, what it costs now, who used
it and what happened, when you need it, when you absolutely do not. This is
the "break down how the AI actually works" request, model tuning included,
grounded in cases instead of theory.

### C. REALITY CHECK (recurring flavor, every week or two)

The anti hype desk. The 95 percent stat and what the 5 percent do differently.
Why the chatbot pilot died. Agent washing. The compounding error math on long
agent chains. Positioning gold, it builds the trust that makes the case files
believable, and it is the content only an honest shop can afford to publish.

### D. THE TRANSLATION (occasional standalone)

The Alaska translation segment promoted to a full post when the local angle is
strong enough to carry it alone. What SalmonVision style counting means for
Bristol Bay operations. What Indiana's plow route savings would look like
scaled to Anchorage's fleet. Runs on local proof from research/ALASKA_GROUND.md
and always frames honestly, an analysis, not a claim that we built it.

### E. THE FLAGSHIP (phase two)

The week's best case file as a Dispatch style video. Held until the daily
series is proven and the bank is deep.

### The proposed weekly rhythm at daily drafting

Four Case Files, one Capability File, one Reality Check or Translation, and
the seventh slot rotates or rests per the maintainer's posting cadence. The
machine drafts daily either way, the human owns what actually gets posted.

## 6. Lead magnet mechanics (how a post becomes a lead)

The evidence says comment gated giveaways increasingly read as engagement bait
and their effectiveness numbers are vendor sourced. An anti hype brand should
not open with a gimmick. The conversion path, in order,

1. The standing offer, recommended now. Every close slide and caption ends with
   the same calm line pointing at a free field study style analysis for Alaska
   businesses. Wording workshopped at build time, the shape is, we do this
   analysis for Alaska companies, free, if you want your operation looked at
   this way, say so. Zero pressure, infinitely repeatable, compounding
   familiarity. It also happens to be true, which is rare in this genre.
2. The library page, recommended now. A cases section on alaskaaihq.com built
   by the existing site machinery, every shipped case file lives at a stable
   URL, the first comment links it. The site converts through the existing
   services page and the deadline alert email list.
3. The compiled playbook, later. Quarterly, the shipped case files compiled
   into an Anchorage AI Playbook PDF, offered as a plain link, never comment
   gated. A classic magnet done without the bait patina.
4. Keyword comment DM funnels, not recommended. Bait patina, conflicted
   evidence, wrong brand fit.

The quiet compounding asset is the profile funnel, a consistent narrow
expertise signal on the founder's profile with the featured section carrying
the library and the offer. That is where B2B leads actually form.

## 7. Naming

The site already owns a newsroom metaphor, the Docket. The series should join
the same family.

- CASE FILES, recommended. "Alaska AI Case Files, No. 37." Sits naturally
  beside the Docket, implies receipts, numbers well, and the per post unit is
  already called a case.
- Proof of Work, strong as the series tagline rather than the name, "Case
  Files. Proof of work from companies winning with AI."
- How They Did It, plain and honest, weaker as a brand unit.
- The Playbook, generic, every marketer owns one.

## 8. Why this does not collide with the news carousel

Different desk, different surface, different close. The news deck covers the
day's Alaska AI story on the page and closes on the Docket. The case file
teaches one company's win on the founder's profile and closes on the offer.
They cross reference naturally, a news deck about fisheries AI can point at
the fisheries case file and back. Both feed the same site, and the topic
ledgers stay separate because the beats are separate.

## 9. Honest risks, said out loud

- Case supply at daily cadence. 300 plus STRONG cases a year is real pressure.
  Mitigations, the bank amortizes research across runs, flavors B through D
  cut the pure case demand to roughly four a week, an UPDATE reframe is legal
  when a shipped case develops, and the evidence bar never bends to fill a
  slot. If the bank runs thin the machine says so in the draft email instead
  of shipping mush.
- Vendor number contamination. Half the findable mid-market stories are vendor
  published. The rules in config/sources.yaml and the case-critic gate exist
  for exactly this, vendor claims are labeled in the post or the case does not
  ship.
- Salesy drift. The moment the translation slide starts pitching our product
  names, the series dies. The anchoring law from the private repo applies in
  public, recommend what the business genuinely needs, including plain
  software, including nothing.
- Repetition fatigue. Daily case studies can start rhyming. The artwork
  variety ledger, lens rotation, and the flavor rhythm are the countermeasures,
  same machinery that keeps the news decks distinct.
- Slow burn. Lead magnets compound, they do not spike. The measurement that
  matters early is saves, profile visits, and offer replies, not likes.

## 10. What the research bank already holds

research/CASE_BANK.md carries the first verified inventory of mid-market wins
with sources and skepticism labels, research/ALASKA_GROUND.md carries the
local proof layer (SalmonVision counting fish at 16 Alaska and BC sites, NOAA

## 11. The recommendation, in one paragraph

Build the Case File daily carousel on the proven studio architecture, run it
on the founder's profile with the page as archive, rotate in Capability Files
and Reality Checks weekly, close every post with the standing offer and a
first comment that carries the sources and the library link. Ship the video
flagship weekly in phase two once the bank is deep. Name it Case Files, tag it
proof of work. This uses every asset the shop already owns, matches what the
platform data says wins, teaches what the adoption research says is true, and
converts the only way this brand credibly can, by doing the work in public.

## 12. The decision list (maintainer picks, then we build)

1. Direction. The Case File core as recommended, or a different mix from the
   menu.
2. Surface. Founder profile with page archive (recommended), or page only.
3. Cadence. Machine drafts daily regardless. Post daily, or weekdays, or 3 to
   5 a week per the platform evidence.
4. The standing offer wording and whether it names the free study explicitly.
5. Merge policy once the routine exists, autonomous like the siblings, or
   review first during a trust period.
6. The name.

## 13. The funnel, end to end (added after the build)

Post (proof, daily) then save or profile visit (the metrics that matter)
then the profile funnel (headline, featured section carrying the library
and the offer) then an offer reply in comments or DM (the conversion event)
then the free field study conversation (the leadflow motion, human owned)
then the build. Nothing in the machine sends, sells, or chases. The post
supplies proof, the profile supplies the door, the human walks through it.

Measurement, honestly. Lead magnets compound, they do not spike. Watch
saves, profile visits, and offer replies at 48 hours and weekly, ignore
likes. The aftercare checklist in every draft carries this so the habit
sticks. If saves are flat after 20 runs the thesis slide order changes
before anything else does (identification before mechanism is the bet).

## 14. The compounding assets (the moat grows every run)

- The bank (ledger/bank.json) compounds research, restocked by scouts,
  never starting from zero.
- The Anchorage bottleneck map (knowledge/ALASKA_TRANSLATION.md) gains a
  proven row per run and goes public at 15 plus rows as the definitive
  local artifact.
- The case library page on alaskaaihq.com gives every post a permanent
  citable home (phase 1.5, wired from the carousels repo).
- The instincts and upgrades ledgers make the machine measurably better
  every run, the same self improvement loop the news studio runs.
- Killed Files, an occasional radical honesty edition publishing the cases
  we REJECTED and why (evidence collapsed, vendor only, could not
  corroborate). Nobody else in this genre can afford to publish their
  rejects. Optional, powerful, maintainer's call.
- The Saltwater Inc interview lead and its successors turn the series from
  citing Outside proof to producing first party Alaska proof.

## Addendum, 2026-07-21, the build shipped

This document began as a brainstorm. The recommendation was then built all
the way through in the same session, the engine vendored and proven
